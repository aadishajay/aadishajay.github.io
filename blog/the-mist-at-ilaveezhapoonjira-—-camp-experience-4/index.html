The Mist at Ilaveezhapoonjira — Camp Experience

Published on 18 Dec 2018

Ilaveezhapoonjira — the name in Malayalam means the pond to which no leaves fall . Situated approx. 10 kms from Kanjar in Kottayam District of Kerala, this location is known for its serene beauty of the natural pond located at 1500 mts surrounded by 3 hills.

LetsGoForACamp.com planned for a weekend camping in Ilaveezhapoonjira. The Camp “TheMist” was conducted on 15–16 December 2018. We gathered 19 registrations and set off to the journey. The campers met at Kanjar , a few came from Bengaluru via Thodupuzha and the rest all were from various parts of Kerala. Plan was to meet at Kanjar at 9.00 am on 15 Dec 2018, Saturday. Almost everyone made thereon time, except a few due to traffic.

Jeeps were arranged from Kanjar. The on-timers left in two jeeps and another waited for those who couldn’t make it on time.   The jeep ride from Kanjar to Ilaveezhapoonjira itself is a feast to eyes. With greenery all around and exhilarating view of the Western Ghats , the asphalted road then slowly turns into one that is filled with cobble stones , indicating a soon to be pucca road or a construction plan that is paused. One can enjoy the comfortable ride of a car until the village of Chakkikavu , after which an off road capable jeep is a must.   Soon the cobblestones too disappear and then there is dirt road. The stay was in a property atop one of the hills, the owners of which have tried to maintain the aesthetics of traditional Kerala homes — two tile roofed dormitories and a bamboo house with bamboo beds.

The way to our stay — the bamboo hut

Team started with an introductory session about Ilaveezhapoonjira and then proceeded to ice-breaking games.   Much awaited lunch was served — Kerala style rice, sambar, fish curry and veg fries. A short rest was followed by a group activity — each team was to enact group of a wild animal each, which turned out to be hilarious with a group imitating sloths. A game to beat the afternoon laziness and to warm up before the trek was also played.

Ice Breaker — Games

The evening trek was to the iconic pond — Ilavezhapoonjira. We understood that the phenomenon of no leaves in the pond is in fact due to the high altitude and the heavy winds, which makes growth of any broad leaf vegetation impossible around the pond.

Then the team proceeded to Muniyara Cave. Locals’ belief is that monks used to meditate in this natural cave. It is located some 50 feet down from the road.The cave is filled with a foot of water, is around 10 feet high, and can be walked in for some 50 feet. Our guide — Achu told that the cave was actually longer to walk, but a recent landslide has blocked the way.

On the way back, with the sun setting up the reddish orange hue, our guide decided it is time for some adventure. Therefore, the team started to climb up a hill deviating from the road. Ilaveezhaponjira , like most of the high hills in Western Ghats is covered by rolling grasslands. We started walking uphill,making our own way and guided solely by the direction to our campsite. Original plan was to reach the camping site by dusk , but time had different plans.   We could cover only 70% by dusk . Luckily for us , almost everyone had flashlights with them. The remainder of the journey was less challenging except for being dark as we had already reached atop a hill and what was left was to walk atop another which stood taller but the col between them was not too deep.

However, when we reached a ridge we could see the beauty of the night, with stars above our heads and on our two sides the city lights of Palai and Thodupuzha. This made us feel that the tiring trek was worth taking.

Once back to campsite, the participants had a discussion on the Western Ghats ,its peculiarities , the challenges it is facing and also the grasslands and their importance contrary to popular belief that they are trivial.   Camp fire was waiting us , with the cold wind slowly building up from the hills. Apart from the many songs sung in different languages , some of the participants shared about some of their unforgettable travel experiences. Vivek, a teacher from Calicut explained how the journey he was just back from , the Youth Integration Program to Punjab , helped to change the outlook of the youngster who went with him. Arun Balan shared his experiences he had from a Wayanad camp, where he lost his way. Riya talked about her journey she embarked to Manali from Patna , and was adventurous with some serious turn of events. The tiresome trek called for some deep sleep , as the next day would start early.

The next morning the campers woke up around 5.45 am and assembled to see the sunrise on a rock near the bamboo hut. The fog and cloud were masking the sun and the wait was for long. Meanwhile , the campers had a short stint of silent listening to all the sounds around , the birds , the breeze and also devotional songs from a far away temple. Finally , we could get a gaze of the sun , between two clouds.

Black tea was ready and so was the next activity. Each of the three teams were now to do some creative work on papers distributed to them. This brought out the writers , poets , doodlers , painters and even sand artists. Each team also had a summary of their experiences written by their group leaders.

Breakfast arrived at the campsite — Appam and Veg Stew and was finished in a jiffy. Everyone was now ready for the trek , which doubles up as return journey as well. With all the packing done and essentials for the trek separated into a small bag, everyone’s backpacks would be sent transported down to the trek end place in jeeps.

Achu , our guide led the pack. We started trekking up the first hill, again with no definite path to follow. The top was a flat rock which gave a 360 degree view of the surrounding hills.

The next hill was the most challenging, as we had to climb up a steep rock, which was a real test of endurance and courage. With some mutual help and helping hands of the guide, everyone of the team made it. It was a moment of self realization for many when the mount was conquered.

The trekking through the grasslands were less tiring , thanks to the wind that soothed us. We had a the camp review en-route on a rock.The reviews were highly positive along with a few suggestions as well.   The col after the second hill was also the path of an ephemeral stream , it had deep inundations on the ground. Atop the third hill was a hold cross , hence a colloquial name is Kurishumala. Being a Via Crucis , there was a path leading us down to the foothills.

We walked through this route until a water tap . This route was evergreen forest , typical to the Ghats.   The team reached the village of Chakkikavu , where our return jeeps to were waiting. We bade goodbye to the marvelous trekking destination of Ilaveezhapoonjira , with lots of memories about the camp.
